# A product video from stills and voice

The video is made of a few generated pictures, each shown while its line is spoken, with subtitles. No moving footage, so the plan has to carry it: one clear promise, pictures that show the product, lines that are easy to say.

## 1. One promise

Before the storyboard, write:

- The product, in the client's words, and the three facts about it that are certain (what it is, what it does, what it costs or where to get it).
- The viewer: who, and what they are doing when this plays (scrolling with sound off, mostly).
- The one thing they should remember.

Facts come from the brief. A missing price or feature is left out, not invented.

## 2. Structure (four scenes, about 6 seconds each)

1. **Hook**: the problem or the result, in the viewer's words. The product is visible in the first picture.
2. **What it is**: the product, close, in use. One feature that matters.
3. **Proof**: a detail that can be seen (material, size, before and after, what is in the box). Not a claim.
4. **Call to action**: where to get it and what to do now, with the product and its name.

For a longer film, repeat scene 2 or 3; never add a scene without a new fact.

## 3. The storyboard, in this form

```
Style line: <one sentence fixing the look of every picture: setting, light, colour palette, camera>
Product line: <one sentence describing the product exactly the same way every time: shape, colour, material, size, distinctive mark>

Scene 1 - Hook
  Voice-over: <one or two short sentences, about 14 words>
  Picture: <subject, action, framing>
  On-screen text: <at most 5 words, or none>
...
```

The style line and the product line are repeated word for word in every picture prompt: that is what keeps four separate pictures looking like one film.

## 4. Voice-over lines

- Written for the ear: short words, no brackets, no abbreviations a voice would stumble on. Numbers as they are spoken ("dua ratus ribu rupiah").
- About 2.3 words a second: 6 seconds is 12 to 15 words.
- One idea a line. The first line works with the sound off, because the subtitles carry it.
- No "Introducing", no "Are you tired of". Start with the thing.

## 5. Picture prompts

Each prompt is one self-contained paragraph for an image generator that knows nothing about the project:

- Start with the product line, then the scene: what is shown, where, what is happening.
- Framing: the frame is cropped after generation. Keep the product and anything important in the middle of the picture, with empty margin all round; nothing important at the edges.
- Light, palette and camera from the style line.
- Ask for no text, no logos, no watermarks in the picture: generated lettering is wrong. Titles are added afterwards.
- No real people's likenesses, no brand marks the client did not supply.
- A generated picture of a product is an illustration. Say in your summary that real product photos should replace them where the look of the product matters.

## 6. Check before handing over

- Does each scene add a fact? Could one be cut?
- Read the lines aloud with a timer.
- Is the product line identical in every prompt?
- Would the video make sense with the sound off? With the pictures off?
- Is every claim in the brief?
