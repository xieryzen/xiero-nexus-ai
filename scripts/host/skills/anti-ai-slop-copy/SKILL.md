# Copy that does not sound machine-made

A reader decides in a sentence whether a text was written for them. Write the sentence only this business could write.

## Start from facts

- List what you know for certain: what is sold, the price, what is included, who made it, where, how long it takes, the guarantee. Write from that list.
- A gap is marked `TODO(client): ...`. Do not invent a fact, a number, a customer quote, an award or a deadline.
- Write in the language of the brief. For Indonesian: natural everyday Indonesian of the audience (formal "Anda" for business, "kamu" for young consumer brands; decide once and keep it), no translated-English sentence shapes.

## Words and shapes that give the machine away: cut them

English: "elevate", "unlock", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced world", "whether you're ... or ...", "not just X, but Y", "look no further", "take it to the next level", "we understand that".

Indonesian: "solusi terbaik", "terpercaya dan profesional", "di era digital ini", "tidak hanya ... tetapi juga ...", "wujudkan impian Anda", "berkualitas tinggi dengan harga terjangkau", "tunggu apa lagi", "menjadi pilihan tepat", "kami hadir untuk".

Shapes: three adjectives in a row; every paragraph ending in a slogan; a question the reader did not ask as an opener; exclamation marks; emoji as bullets; headings that are puns; a summary that repeats what was just said; lists of exactly three for no reason; bold on every other phrase.

## What to write instead

- **Concrete beats grand.** "Dikirim dari Bandung, sampai 2 hari di Jawa" instead of "pengiriman cepat". A number, a name, a material, a place.
- **One idea a sentence.** Short sentences, plain verbs. Read it aloud: if you would not say it to a customer at the counter, rewrite it.
- **Lead with what the buyer gets**, then how, then proof. Put the price or the next step where the eye lands.
- **Proof that can be checked**: a photo, a spec, a guarantee with its terms, a real review with a name the client supplied.
- **Say the limits.** What it does not do, who it is not for, what costs extra. That is what makes the rest believable.
- **Buttons and labels say what happens next**: "Bayar sekarang", "Cek ongkir", "Unduh brosur (PDF, 2 MB)".
- **Error messages help**: what went wrong, in the user's words, and what to do now.

## Formats

- Headline: at most 10 words; what it is and for whom.
- Product description: 1 line what it is; 3 to 5 lines of specifics (size, material, what is in the box, compatibility); 1 line of care or warranty. No superlatives.
- Voice-over: write for the ear. Words that are easy to say, numbers as they are spoken, about 2.3 words a second.
- Caption: first line carries the point (it is all that shows before "more"); one call to action; 3 to 8 hashtags that people really search, no walls of tags.

## Before you hand it in

1. Delete the first sentence. Is the text better? Keep it deleted.
2. Search for every word in the lists above.
3. Check every number and name against the brief.
4. Could a competitor paste this on their site unchanged? Then it says nothing yet.

When you review someone else's text, quote the phrase, name the rule it breaks, and give the rewritten line.
