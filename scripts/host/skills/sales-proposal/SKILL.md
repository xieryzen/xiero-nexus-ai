# Proposals and quotations

A client says yes when they can see their problem understood, know exactly what they will get and when, and are not afraid of a surprise. Write for that; it is not a brochure.

## 1. Before writing

From the brief, list:

- The client's situation and the result they want, in their words.
- What you know about volume, deadline and budget, and what you do not know.
- What the provider really offers: services, prices, turnaround, guarantees. **Prices, names, legal details and dates come from the brief.** A missing one is `TODO(provider): ...`; you may suggest a structure for a price, never the number as if it were agreed.

Questions that change the scope are asked at the top, each with the assumption you used so the proposal is still usable.

## 2. Structure (two to four pages)

1. **Summary**: the need, the proposed solution, the price range or total, the start date. A reader who stops here knows the offer.
2. **What you asked for**: their goal and constraints, restated. Shows it was read.
3. **What we will do**: the scope as deliverables a client can check ("Panel and Wings installed on your VPS, one node, one test server running"), not activities ("consulting").
4. **What is not included**: the items clients assume. Each with its price or "on request".
5. **Options**: at most three packages, differing in things the client understands (scope, speed, support time). Name them by what they are, not Bronze / Silver / Gold. Mark the one you recommend and why.
6. **Timeline**: steps with durations, and what you need from the client at each (access, content, approval). The clock starts when those arrive.
7. **Price and payment**: the amount, currency and tax treatment; payment schedule (for example 50 % to start, 50 % on handover); payment methods; how long the offer is valid.
8. **Terms that prevent disputes**: number of revisions; what counts as done (acceptance); support period and channel; what happens when the scope changes; ownership of the work; confidentiality; cancellation.
9. **Next step**: one action ("Balas 'setuju paket B' ke nomor ini") and what happens within a day of it.

## 3. Rules

- Plain words. A client who has to ask what a term means will not sign.
- Numbers: rupiah as `Rp 1.500.000`; say whether tax is included; totals add up (check the arithmetic).
- Promises only the provider can keep: no "100 % uptime", no "guaranteed first page".
- Risks stated with what is done about them: honesty here is what makes the rest believable.
- Proof that is real: similar work the provider actually did, supplied in the brief. No invented clients or testimonials.
- The same service has the same name everywhere in the document.
- For technical services (servers, panels, plugins): say what access is needed and how it is handled (who holds passwords, that they are changed after handover).

## 4. A quotation table

| No | Item | What it includes | Qty | Unit price | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then subtotal, discount with its reason, tax, total, and the validity date.

## 5. Check before sending

- Could the client tell exactly what they get, by when, for how much, and what is extra?
- Does every number come from the brief or carry a TODO?
- Do the totals add up?
- Is there one next step?
- Read it as the client's cautious partner: what would they object to? Answer it in the text.
