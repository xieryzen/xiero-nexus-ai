# Prompts for pictures that belong together

The generator knows nothing about the project and makes each picture from its prompt alone. Consistency comes from repeating the same words; clarity comes from saying what is in the frame.

## The prompt, in this order

1. **Subject**: what it is, exactly, the same way every time: shape, colour, material, size, the one distinctive detail. Copy this sentence unchanged into every prompt of the set.
2. **What is happening and where**: the action or arrangement, the surface or place.
3. **Framing**: how close, from which angle, where the subject sits in the frame.
4. **Light**: source, direction, hard or soft, time of day.
5. **Palette and style**: three colours at most by name; the kind of picture (studio product photograph, editorial still life, flat illustration). Copy this unchanged into every prompt of the set.
6. **What to leave out**: "no text, no lettering, no logos, no watermark, no people" as the task requires.

One paragraph, plain words, no lists, no references to other pictures, tasks or scenes ("as before" means nothing to the generator).

## Framing for pictures that will be cropped

Pictures are generated square (1024 by 1024) and often shown cropped to a vertical or wide frame, or with text set on them.

- Keep the subject and everything that matters in the central area, with clear margin on all four sides.
- For a vertical crop, nothing important near the left and right edges. For a wide crop, nothing important near the top and bottom.
- Where words will be added later, ask for a calm, empty area and say where ("the upper third is plain background").

## What generators get wrong: plan around it

- **Lettering**: words in a picture come out misspelled. Never ask for text, labels, prices or logos in the picture; they are set afterwards.
- **Brands and real people**: do not ask for a real brand's mark or a real person's likeness unless the client supplied it and has the right to it.
- **Hands, small text, exact counts**: avoid close-ups of hands holding things, and "exactly five" of anything.
- **The actual product**: a generated picture is an illustration of a product like it, not the product. Say in your summary which pictures should be replaced by real photographs before publishing.

## A set

Write one style block and one subject block first, then one prompt per picture that starts with the subject block and ends with the style block. Vary only the middle: the action, the place, the framing.

Check the set: the same adjectives, the same light, the same palette words in every prompt; no prompt that depends on another.

## Examples of the difference

Weak: "A nice photo of coffee for the brochure."

Strong: "A matte black 250 g coffee pouch with a round kraft-paper label, standing upright on a pale oak table beside a small pile of roasted beans, seen from slightly above at three-quarter angle, centred with empty space around it, soft daylight from a window on the left, warm neutral palette of black, oak and cream, studio product photograph with shallow depth of field, no text, no lettering, no logos, no people."
